# S2. Scope-screening protocol

Screening used title and abstract text. Cluster labels were descriptive only;
they did not decide inclusion.

## In-scope objects

A record was eligible when AI was central to scholarly or scientific writing,
manuscripts, authorship, references, scholarly publishing, editorial work,
journals, peer review, research integrity, disclosure, AI-text detection,
paper mills, evidence-review work, or institutional academic-AI rules tied to
research, writing, assessment, or integrity.

## Exclusions

Records centred on clinical diagnosis, imaging, treatment, patient outcomes,
industrial or engineering applications, general educational technology, or
language learning were excluded. Domain words alone did not decide a record.
For example, AI-assisted medical writing could be included, while a
clinical-AI study that mentioned a review method was excluded.

## Decision order

1. Exclude retracted records, records outside 2020--2026, and records without
   a usable abstract.
2. Mark positive scholarly-workflow signals and negative off-topic signals.
3. Include records with positive signals and an AI signal when no negative
   signal applies. Exclude records with only negative signals.
4. Resolve mixed cases by the central object of the paper. Writing and
   publishing could outweigh a medical-domain term when the object was
   scholarly communication. Workflow or evidence-synthesis terms did not
   outweigh a clinical-AI object.
5. Send unresolved cases to manual review. Eight unresolved records were kept
   out of all reported analyses.

Screening was conducted by one reviewer. The package records the rules and
aggregate decisions; it does not claim independent screening reliability.
